— Primary Threats

What's Killing Our Wildlife

Human activity drives the sixth mass extinction. The causes are interconnected and accelerating — but largely reversible, if we act now.

01

Habitat Destruction

Logging, agriculture, and urban sprawl erase ecosystems at a rate of 27 football fields every minute.

02

Poaching & Illegal Trade

The black market for wildlife is worth $23 billion annually — animals slaughtered for horns, pelts, and scales.

03

Climate Disruption

Shifting seasons and warming habitats outpace evolution. Species that took millennia to adapt cannot survive decades of rapid change.

04

Pollution

Plastic waste, agricultural runoff, and industrial toxins contaminate every ecosystem — from mountain peaks to ocean floors.

05

Human-Wildlife Conflict

As wild spaces shrink, animals venture into human territory. The result is lethal — especially for wildlife.

06

Invasive Species

Species introduced to new environments overwhelm native biodiversity, reshaping entire food webs and erasing endemic life.

— The Crisis in Time

A History of Loss

1900

The Last Passenger Pigeon

Martha, the last known passenger pigeon, died in captivity at Cincinnati Zoo. Once numbering in the billions, her species was driven to extinction in decades by overhunting and habitat loss.

1973

Endangered Species Act Signed

The United States passes landmark legislation offering legal protection to threatened and endangered species — a model that inspired conservation law worldwide.

1992

Convention on Biological Diversity

196 nations sign the CBD at the Rio Earth Summit, pledging to protect biodiversity, use biological resources sustainably, and share benefits equitably.

2020

WWF Living Planet Report

A devastating report reveals a 68% average decline in monitored wildlife populations between 1970 and 2016 — the most comprehensive record of Earth's accelerating biodiversity crisis.

2022

Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework

Nations commit to protecting 30% of Earth's land and oceans by 2030 — an ambitious but essential pledge to halt and reverse nature loss.

— Take Action

How You Can Help

Donate to Conservation

Support organisations like WWF, Wildlife Conservation Society, and African Wildlife Foundation. Even small monthly contributions fund rangers, research, and habitat restoration.

Choose Responsibly

Avoid products that drive deforestation — check for sustainable palm oil, FSC-certified wood, and ethical seafood certifications when you shop.

Spread Awareness

Share information, engage with conservation campaigns, and encourage friends and family to care about the biodiversity crisis. Public pressure moves policy.

Support Wildlife Corridors

Advocate for the protection and restoration of land corridors connecting fragmented habitats — allowing animal populations to migrate, breed, and recover.

Stay Informed

Monthly updates on conservation news, success stories, and ways to help.
